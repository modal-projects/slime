🔬 Assessment

Why the idea is promising

Eight continuations from the exact same state form a cleaner comparison than eight independent episodes. They condition out prefix variance, reuse expensive tool work, and directly expose whether the policy can improve or repair a concrete partial solution. Frontier-CS is suitable because it has continuous scores, iterative submissions, and observable regressions.

What must be claimed carefully

A past snapshot changes the reset-state distribution. This is not an unbiased policy gradient for episodes starting from the original problem. It is a mixed reset curriculum: suffix actions are on-policy, but states are sampled from a recent replay distribution. Start-state evaluation must remain the scientific endpoint.

🧩 Minimal algorithm

Training mixture

Keep 32 groups fixed: 24 fresh and 8 retro. Each retro group restores one snapshot into eight independent sandboxes. Siblings share group_index but retain distinct sample and rollout IDs.

On-policy suffix, masked prefix

Reconstruct conditioning exactly, loss-mask the inherited prefix, and record logprobs only for new suffix tokens. Pin siblings to one behavior version; cap source age at four updates and monitor importance-ratio ESS.

One simple reward

Train on final fractional verifier score. Log improvement over snapshot and branch-local best, but do not shape on them initially or inherit the parent’s best submission.

Same remaining horizon

Give each sibling only the parent’s remaining step and wall-time budget. A branch cannot receive 75 fresh turns from a 70%-complete state without changing compute and horizon.

Final reward is the right first version
Within one sibling group, subtracting the snapshot score from every final reward is an additive constant. GRPO centering removes it, so absolute final reward and improvement reward produce the same normalized advantages. Train on final reward; log improvement for analysis.

Formal objective: optimize 0.75 × start-state return + 0.25 × return from the selected recent-state distribution. This is a deliberate reset curriculum, not an unchanged start-state objective.

🌿 Which trajectories and turns should branch?
Choose branchable states, not merely good or bad trajectories
Good or solved states often make every sibling succeed; arbitrary bad states often make every sibling fail. Both yield zero-variance groups. The useful target is a state whose current-policy continuations are likely to diverge—especially a repairable state or an unsolved state with demonstrated progress and budget left.

Take at most one snapshot per parent episode, immediately after tool result and observation commit, never while Sandbox.exec is running. Reset local submission logs and assign a new AGENT_ID to each sibling.

Why not fixed 50%, 70%, or ΔBelief? Fixed percentages ignore whether a useful transition occurred and are undefined until final trajectory length is known. ΔBelief needs likelihood of a unique target answer; Frontier-CS admits many valid programs. Log NLL and first-token entropy as exploratory features, not the first selector.

🗺️ Three-phase experiment plan
Phase 0 · snapshot fidelity and cost smoke

Run 20 filesystem and 20 directory snapshots across small and large workspaces. Restore eight siblings and verify independent edit, compile, submit, and grade behavior.

Proceed only with 100% state/prompt fidelity, no cross-sibling contamination, and small p95 snapshot-plus-restore overhead.

Phase 1 · branchability survey

Collect 64 eligible snapshots and eight current-policy continuations each. Stratify one survey across random-middle, promising-unsolved, recovery, and high-surprisal candidates.

Do not train until retro groups produce useful reward contrast at cost no worse than fresh groups.

Phase 2 · compute-matched training A/B

Start both arms from the same clean checkpoint and fingerprint. Replace 8 of 32 fresh groups with retro groups; reward strategy is the sole difference.

Evaluate all 38 Frontier-CS problems at step 0 and every five steps. Continue beyond the pilot only on held-out efficiency improvement.

Decision rule

What success looks like

Advance beyond the pilot only if retro replay improves full held-out Frontier-CS reward AUC or solve rate per GPU-hour, while increasing informative-group yield and measured recovery, with no material degradation in asynchronous staleness, submission load, stability, or USACO transfer.

The strongest research story is not “snapshots make rollouts cheaper.” It is that recent, repairable states define a useful auxiliary reset distribution that teaches an asynchronous coding agent to recover and continue optimizing—and that the benefit survives evaluation from the original problem start.
